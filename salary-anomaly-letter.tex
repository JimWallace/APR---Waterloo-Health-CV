\opening{Dear Dean Liu,}

\textbf{Re: Request for consideration for a salary anomaly adjustment}

I ask to be considered for a salary anomaly adjustment from the Faculty's Anomalies Fund, which the Memorandum of Agreement establishes ``to correct individual salary anomalies'' (MoA 13.3.3(d)). The Faculty's Performance Review Guidelines provide that these adjustments ``are determined by the Dean based upon recommendations of the Chair/Directors''; the Director has referred my case to you. I enclose my analysis, which uses only the University's public salary disclosures for 2023 to 2025 and can be repeated exactly.

\textbf{My salary is at the 2nd percentile of recent promotions to Professor.} I was promoted to Professor on July 1, 2025. Among all 48 research-stream faculty on the main campus who were promoted from Associate Professor to Professor in 2024 or 2025, my annualized base salary of \$162,629 ranks 47th of 48. It is \$45,328 (22\%) below the group's median of \$207,957, and \$26,704 below its lower quartile of \$189,333.

\textbf{Differences between Faculties do not explain it.} Of the six members of the group in the Faculty of Health, my salary is the lowest. Within the School of Public Health Sciences, all 11 other Professors were paid more in 2025 than my annualized base, as were 10 of the 14 Associate Professors.

\textbf{The gap predates my promotion.} In 2024, when all 22 members of my promotion cohort were Associate Professors, my disclosed salary was the lowest of the 22. The University's decision to promote me means that my record meets its standard for the rank, so a salary at the 2nd percentile of recent promotions is difficult to explain by differences in performance.

\textbf{The comparison is conservative.} My figure is an annualized base salary, because my disclosed 2025 salary includes about eight months of sabbatical at 85\% pay. The 2025 figures for colleagues promoted that year include about six months at the Associate Professor rate, which tends to understate the difference. The analysis is descriptive: it does not identify the cause of the gap. It is consistent, however, with my 2026 analysis of salaries in SPHS from 2011 to 2025 (doi:10.5281/zenodo.24397928), which found that faculty affiliated with the Master of Health Informatics program are paid below the School's median far more often than other faculty, and that their salaries grow more slowly.

\textbf{My request.} I ask for a permanent adjustment of \$30,000 (18\%) to my base salary, which would bring it to \$192,629: between the lower quartile and the median of the University comparison group, and near the lower edge of the middle half of SPHS Professors. I would be glad to discuss the analysis, and to provide its data and code.

\closing{Sincerely,}
